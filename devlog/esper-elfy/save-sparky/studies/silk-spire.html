The Silk Spire

A level, not a diorama: one realm of the climb, with its one boss. Elfy arrives from below, up the steps from the bridge she came by. She meets Nana Nightloom, a knitting granny spider who walks the floor, the walls and the ceiling. She wins, the web over the hatch unravels, and she climbs out to the terrace, where the realm's gate is its checkpoint and the next realm hangs above her, lit gold. The game is still a flat 2D plane (the inset). The world around it is built in depth all the way out: a colonnade you see straight through, floating stairs and islands, towers, a city of spires in the fog, a sea of cloud far below. Everything is drawn by code.

  • The camera trick. Instead of lowering the camera to see the ceiling, the camera's eye rides at Elfy's height with a gentle perspective. Anything above her eye shows its underside, so the ceiling Nana walks on is always in view while Elfy is below it. Anything below shows its top, so the floor she stands on, and the steps she came up, read as surfaces. Nothing tilts, and her controls never change.
  • The plane is 1:1. The camera's distance equals its focal length, so the play plane renders at exactly one world unit per pixel. Elfy, Nana, the platforms' front edges and every warning are crisp, unscaled pixel art. Only things in front of or behind the plane grow or shrink.
  • Walls and ceilings in 2D. In the inset, the chamber is a box of lines: floor, two walls, a ceiling with the hatch, two ledges. Nana is a red square that walks all of them; her lap is one path round the box. On screen she is drawn in her own frame (her surface below her), then turned onto the floor, a wall or the ceiling.
  • No void. The spire is open: a colonnade behind the chamber, and below it a bare framework of pillars, beams and hanging lanterns, so the world shows through. Around it, at every depth: two towers and a bridge, flights of floating stairs, small islands with lit houses, a city of spires, an aqueduct, a cloud sea. Fog thickens with distance, so it fades instead of ending, and everything slides past at its own rate as the camera rises.
  • Where you came from, where you are going. At the start the camera is low: the terrace, the long bridge off into the fog, the bathhouse far down it (the last realm), and the chamber's underside above. At the end it rises past the terrace to the gate, the checkpoint, and the next realm's golden island overhead.
  • One enemy, once. A realm has its boss; when she is beaten, its top opens (here, the web over the hatch unravels), and the gate at the top is the save point. An enemy you left unbeaten could follow you up through that hatch into the next realm, so two fights overlap.
