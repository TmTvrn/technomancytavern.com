Realm 9: The Midas Night Bazaar

A night market in a canyon, under a colossal golden hand whose fingers pour molten gold in two glowing falls. Striped stalls, paper lanterns, gold in the cracks of the cobbles, stall-roof ledges either side and, in the middle, a great merchant's scale whose pans tilt with whatever lands on them. Gold here is molten and stretchy: it sags, pulls, snaps and drips. The shopkeepers are goofy hybrids from mixed-up lore, drawn like 1930s rubber-hose cartoons: noodle limbs, white gloves, pie-cut eyes and big brows that do all the acting, with hand-lettered sound words on the big hits. Realm 8: the last bathhouse.

1 · the Beckon, the Charge, the Money Rain, the Slip · molten gold, a lucky cat

Lucky Bull

A minotaur who runs a lucky-charm stall, crossed with the beckoning cat: a big brown bull in a red happi coat trimmed in gold, rubber-hose arms in white gloves, one paw forever raised and waving you in. Gold bells on his horns, a gold ring through his nose, a gold tooth in his grin, and the bushiest brows in the bazaar.

idle
  • The beckon. His paw waves, his brows go sly, and on each wave a spinning coin arcs out onto a ring. One lands on the left pan of the scale, which tips with the weight. Elfy gets up onto the stall roof.
  • The charge. Warning: he paws the cobbles, brows slammed down, snorting dust, and the lane lights up. Then he runs the length of the bazaar into the stall under Elfy. CLANG.
  • Stuck. His horns go into the molten gold of the stall; he tugs, eyes worried, teeth gritted, and the gold stretches and sags and thins until it snaps, BOING, and flings him back across the market, squashing as he lands.
  • The money rain (phase two). He shakes his head, the horn bells ring, and coins fall out of the dark onto rings one after another, ledge, floor, pan, floor. Keep moving: down off the roof and up onto the scale.
  • The slip, and the weak window. He strolls over to gloat, steps on one of his own coins, and goes over flat on his back, hooves in the air, stars circling. Elfy hits him three times; he dusts himself off and waves you back in.
2 · the Wheel, the Weighing, the Bonus Round, the Showcase, Bankrupt · gold, a game show

Anubis, Live!

The weigher of hearts, hosting a game show: a black jackal head with tall gold-lined ears and a striped headdress, gold kohl wings round his pie-cut eyes and gold brows over them, a lapis tuxedo, a red bow tie, a broad gold collar, and a gold microphone always at his snout. His wheel picks the round. His scale decides who is guilty.

idle
  • Spin the wheel. His free arm is rubber hose: it stretches the length of the market to flick the wheel, which ticks round, slows, and stops with the heart wedge under the pointer.
  • The weighing. He tosses a heart onto one pan and lets a feather drift onto the other; the heart is heavier, so the scale tips, and the heavy half of the floor lights up in outlined ankh columns. Get to the light side.
  • WRONG! The buzzer; the columns fill solid gold and slam down. Then the bonus round: he stretches out, moves the heart to the other pan, and the other half is guilty. Run back.
  • The showcase (phase two). He spins the wheel so hard it shakes on its stand, the floor lane flickers, and it pops off, BOING, drops to the cobbles and rolls the bazaar wall to wall, BONK, and back. Stand on the raised pan; Elfy's weight softens the tilt.
  • Bankrupt, the weak window. One more spin, just for him. Black wedge. His jaw drops, his ears droop, his collar falls off, and he sits down on his roof and cries. Elfy jumps up and hits him three times; he rewinds the wheel onto its stand and straightens his tie.
3 · the Riddle, the Geysers, the Rainbow, the Lucky Coin · gold, a pot of it

The Sphinxrechaun

A sphinx crossed with a leprechaun: a tawny lion's body lying along a stall roof like a temple statue, a little round rosy face in a huge ginger beard-and-mane, ginger brows like hedges, a tall plum top hat with a gold buckle, and a clay pipe. He guards three pots of gold with a riddle, and breathes rainbows with the green left out (Elfy has it).

idle
  • The riddle. He raises a paw, brows sly, and his speech bubble shows a cyan gem. Three pots of gold stand on the floor, each with a colored band. The answer is the cyan one.
  • The geysers. Warning: the two wrong pots rattle under outlined columns. Then FOOSH: molten gold shoots out of them to the sky and pours back in strands. Stand by the right pot.
  • The rainbow. He puffs his cheeks, the arch is outlined, then he breathes a real rainbow (red, orange, gold, cyan, blue, violet; no green) whose foot sweeps the whole floor in sparks. Jump it as it passes.
  • The lucky coin (phase two). He flips his coin on his paw, misses, and his face falls. MY COIN! He leaps off his roof and gallops after it the length of the bazaar, straight through anything in the way. Get up on the scale, then onto his empty roof.
  • Stuck, the weak window. The coin rolls into his biggest pot; he dives in after it and sticks head-first, legs kicking, tail wagging, the pot rattling. Elfy hits him three times; POP, out he comes with the coin and bounds home trailing a rainbow.
4 · the Lash, the Aria, the Dive, the Whirl, the Tangle · molten-gold braids, opera

Ninefold Brunhilde

A nine-tailed fox crossed with an opera Valkyrie: a white-and-orange fox face, pie eyes with long lashes and the most dramatic brows in the bazaar, a silver winged helmet with her ears poking through, a silver breastplate, a red skirt, a round gold shield and a spear. Her nine tails are braids of molten gold, and they stretch as far as she likes.

idle
  • The lash. Three braids at a time, each path dashed first: three along the floor (jump), three at head height (crouch), three stabbing down onto rings on the stall, the pan and the floor. The braids stretch and thin as they reach and spring back.
  • The aria. Brows up to her helmet, eyes closed, mouth an O: LA-LA-LAAA. Rings of song spread out from her, each with one gap, and the gap is marked first: stand in it, then jump into it, then get up on the stall into it.
  • The dive (phase two). HO-JO-TO-HO! She raises her spear, her lane flickers, and she dives spear-first into the cobbles, BAM, landing on her feet.
  • The whirl. The circle round her is outlined, then nine braids spin out round her one and a half turns. Stay on the stall, outside it.
  • The tangle, the weak window. She pulls them in, and nine braids tangle: tied up in her own gold in a great bow, she topples, brows furious, lips pouting. Elfy hits her three times; POP, she bursts free in a shower of glints and sings herself home.
5 · the Serenade, the Zapateado, the Sombrero, the Word · cast gold, a mariachi

El Dorado

A golem crossed with a mariachi: a great block of a man cast in gold, in a black charro suit with gold buttons down the trousers, a big red bow tie, a huge black sombrero, a moustache with curled ends, carved brows, and a guitarrón across his belly. A golem lives by the word written on his brow, EMET, truth. Rub out the E and it reads MET, and he stops.

idle
  • The serenade. He sways and sings, and every strum lands on the beat: a gold note head arcs out of the guitarrón onto a ring, four beats, the last one where Elfy was standing. Step off it.
  • The zapateado. Brows down, teeth gritted, he lifts a boot high (the floor lane flickers) and stamps on his roof: the planks bounce and a crest of molten gold runs the length of the cobbles. Three stamps, the last an OLE. Jump each crest.
  • The sombrero (phase two). AY-AY-AY! He grips the brim, and the hat's whole flight is dashed out first: out at head height (crouch), round, back along the floor (jump), and up.
  • The word, the weak window. The sombrero comes home edge-first into his brow, BONK, and smudges the E. MET: a golem without his word stops. Eyes crossed out, he goes stiff and topples off his roof face-down, THOOM. Elfy hits him three times; he rubs the E back on with a gloved thumb and hops home.
6 · More Gold, Bigger, Shinier · the Midas touch, three wishes

The Midas Jinn

King Midas crossed with a genie, the bazaar's last act: a big blue jinn rising on a smoke tail out of a gold lamp, in a gold vest and armbands, pointed ears with gold hoops, a crown-turban with a ruby, a beard of molten gold that drips, pie eyes and enormous black brows. Everything he touches turns to gold. He grants himself three wishes, one a phase.

idle
  • Wish one: MORE GOLD. His rubber-hose arm stretches across the bazaar and a great glove comes down on each outlined spot, SPLANG: the left stall, the left pan, a stretch of the floor. Each turns to molten gold, dripping and glowing, and stays dangerous until it cools. Keep off what he has touched.
  • Wish two: BIGGER. His hands swell to the size of stalls and sweep in from both edges of the bazaar to clap, CLAP, along lanes dashed edge to edge first: low (jump), high (crouch), then tall (get up on the pan, now cool).
  • Wish three: SHINIER (the weak window). He smugly touches his own chest, and the gold spreads out from his fingertips over his whole body until he is a statue of himself, eyes wide, brows up. KLANK. Elfy jumps onto his stall; each hit cracks the gold, and the third shatters the shell, CRACK.
  • The lamp. Blue again and seeing stars, he goes back into his lamp, POOF; it rattles a while, and he rises out of it with three fresh wishes.
