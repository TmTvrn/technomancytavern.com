Realm 6: The Orrery at the End of the Sun

Gravity, time, space and starlight. An abandoned observatory of brass and black glass orbiting a dying red giant: the dome cracked open to space, the bloated crimson sun filling the window, and behind everything a gigantic orrery turning, nested rings and model planets. These are bosses in the hard style: three or four moves each with a distinct tell, a feint that punishes reacting too early, a second phase that changes the room, and punish windows sized to how committed each attack is. The setting is warm, so the enemies are lunar silver and magenta plasma; blades warn in white, projectiles in magenta, gravity in gold. Drawn at the game's pixel scale, with Elfy for size. 1 · Lantern Swing, Kindle, Snuff, Stomp, the Moth Double · solar fire in the dark

The Lamplighter

A stilt-legged automaton three times Elfy's height that wanders the station relighting dead stars: a long black coat in tatters, legs jointed like a heron's, and for a head a brass lantern with a captured sun-ember in it, dust moths always circling. It carries a lamplighter's pole with a burning wick. Its signature is the snuff: it shuts its lantern and the whole room goes out, and you fight it on glints alone.

idle
  • Lantern swing. It rears the burning pole back over its head, leaning away, focus lines closing in, the arc of the swing marked. Then it swings, and the wick leaves a burning arc hanging in the air, dripping embers, that lingers after the swing has passed.
  • Kindle. It spits embers from the lantern onto the ledges and the floor; each lies there pulsing, ringed. They ignite one after another in pillars of solar fire, a line chasing her across the room, and she has to go up.
  • Snuff. It shuts the lantern and the room goes black. All that's left is its moths, its two eye-slits, and the glint at the end of its pole. A glint: nothing comes. That's the feint; reacting to it is what it wants. Then the real glint, she drops, and a thrust runs the whole high ledge in the dark. The lantern opens with a flash.
  • Stomp. It rises on its stilts, the floor marked, then slams down: a wave runs along the floor both ways and the ceiling is shaken loose, debris falling.
  • The moth double (phase two). The moths swarm into a burning copy of it, beside it, that does everything it does half a beat late. It swings; she jumps it; she lands into the copy's swing. Scorched.
  • Weak window. All that fire overheats it: it folds down on its stilts and the lantern swings open to vent steam and embers, the white core exposed.
2 · Track Dash, Derail, Tail Battery, the Split · a serpent on rails

The Ouroboros Engine

A colossal serpent-train of brass carriages that rides the great orrery ring as its rail: a silver locomotive with a skull for a cowcatcher and two magenta lamps for eyes, carriages with little alien passengers glowing at the windows, a missile battery for a tail. Break the right coupling and it splits in two.

idle
  • Track dash. Anticipation the train way: it slows almost to a stop, wheels straining, steam pouring, while chevrons run round the ring and the floor is marked. Then it whips round the ring in a blur, sparks off the rail, leaves it at the bottom and tears off down the floor and out of the room.
  • Derail. Its leap is marked in gold, and it comes back through open space, flying over her; gravity bends toward its body as it passes, gold arrows pulled toward it, and Elfy floats up off her ledge. It lands back on the ring with an impact frame.
  • Tail battery. It coils to a stop. A reticle locks on her; the tail's pods blink; six homing missiles, smoke trails curling. She leads them: off the ledge, under the ring, up the other side, and they come down into the floor and into each other.
  • Weak window and the split (phase two). Overheated, it stalls, its couplings glowing, one white-hot. She dashes into it and it breaks: the two halves race round the ring in opposite directions, the rear half's battery now its face, firing at her. Then they meet at the top and couple again with a clang.
3 · Time Stop, Rewind, the Feint, the Gravity Hour · time and gravity

Madame Perigee, the Astrolabe Witch

A woman's shape made of nested brass and silver rings turning like an astrolabe, round a porcelain face with painted gold lashes and a hairline crack; a magenta clockwork heart ticking inside the rings of her skirt; a clock hand for a wand. She doesn't hit hard. She takes your timing away.

idle
  • Time stop. She raises the wand and fires a fan of clock hands at Elfy, and in the same breath stops time: an impact frame, the world drained grey, the hands hanging in the air with their paths marked ahead of them. Elfy keeps her colour and gets one move: a dash through the gap. Time resumes and the hands drive into the floor where she was.
  • Rewind. A clock ring is traced round her as she climbs to the ledge. Then she's dragged back two seconds along her own grey afterimages, back to where she stood, and the great pendulum was already swinging for that spot. The dodge she made doesn't count; she has to make another, over its blade.
  • The feint. She winds up another time stop exactly as before: focus lines, rings spinning up, arms raised. Elfy braces. Nothing. The witch laughs, her porcelain head rattling. Reacting to the wind-up is what loses.
  • The gravity hour (phase two). A great clock face fills the window. As its hand sweeps up, gold arrows mark which way down is about to be, and gravity flips: everything loose falls up and Elfy lands on the ceiling, running it upside down while needles come up at her. The hand sweeps down, she falls back through a beam marked across the room, and she's time-locked: frozen mid-air, drained grey inside a clock face.
  • Weak window. She overwinds: her rings spin to a scream, throwing sparks, until her mainspring snaps out of her chest and uncoils. She sags to the floor ticking wrong. Then she rewinds herself, back up into the air.
4 · Gravity Mines, the Nuke, Eject · a duo: a tiny pilot and a huge machine

Pilot Quib and the Bulwark

A tiny squid-like alien in a fishbowl helmet steering a hulking siege walker: reverse-jointed legs, a slab of silver hull with magenta running lights, shield pylons on its shoulders, a mortar for an arm and a mine launcher on its back. The walker shields the pilot; the pilot does the thinking. When the walker overheats, Quib ejects and fights on foot.

idle
  • Gravity mines. A hex shield dome goes up over the walker, and the launcher on its back lobs mines; each landing spot is ringed first. They lie pulsing, then each one pulls: gold rings collapsing inward, grit dragged in, Elfy dragged along her ledge. They pop in a chain, and she jumps clear between them.
  • The nuke. The mortar rises, charging, Quib hauling on a lever, focus lines. The blast radius is marked over almost the whole room; the one gap in it is the walker's own shield. She has to run toward it and crouch under the dome. The shell climbs slowly, falls slowly, and the room goes white: a mushroom of fire, three shockwaves, debris bouncing off the dome as it flashes.
  • Eject (phase two). The walker overheats, its hull glowing red and venting; the cockpit pops and Quib ejects on a little jetpack. Now there are two: the empty walker keeps firing bolts at her, and Quib, quick and cowardly, hops up onto a ledge to zap her with a raygun from behind it.
  • Weak window. She dashes up and catches him: a comic star and his fishbowl cracks, water spraying, and he flails on his back, seeing stars. Then he tapes the crack with an X of tape, jets back into the cockpit, and the walker reboots.
5 · One, Two, Four: Tides, Eclipse, New Moon, Grief · four sisters in synergy

The Four Phases

Four alien knight sisters named for the moon, each a different body. New: a shadow-cloaked blink striker, a dark disc for a helm ringed in silver. Crescent: a lithe dancer with two sickles and a crescent horn sweeping off her helm. Half: a squat tank with a half-moon mirror for a shield and a hammer. Full: a tall sniper, her helm a glowing full moon, a lunar lance-rifle. They bow. They fight one at a time, then two, then all four, and their best moves need each other.

idle
  • One. They bow, all four, at their stations. Crescent drops from the high ledge, winds up, sickles out, and spins across the floor as a ring of blades; her line is marked. Elfy jumps her.
  • Two: Tides. Crescent spins on the high ledge and the air bends: wind lines dragging toward Half, and Elfy, running against it, slides toward Half anyway while the hammer goes up, slowly, the floor marked. She jumps out of the pull; the slam sends shockwaves both ways under her.
  • Four: Eclipse and New Moon. Half tilts the half-moon mirror up; Full charges, focus lines, the path of the beam marked from her lens to the mirror and on to Elfy. The beam reflects off the mirror at her and she jumps it. Then New dissolves into motes, a dark mark appears behind Elfy, and New is there, and stabs.
  • Grief. Crescent drops in again, winding up, and Elfy dashes through her first: an impact frame, and Crescent breaks up into motes and drifts away. The other three kneel. Then they rise in a magenta grief-rage aura, eyes white, and roar. Each has something new: New blinks three times down the floor, slashing; Full fires three beams that sweep the high ledge; Half leaps and slams and spikes run down the floor.
  • Weak window. Their formation has a gap: when they swap stations, one of them is alone for a moment. New, caught between blinks, takes the hit and kneels. Then Crescent reforms from the motes, and they return to their stations and bow.
6 · Flares, the Waking, Orbit, the Countdown, Collapse · the final boss of the orrery

The Unborn Sun

At the heart of the orrery, in a cage of magnetic rings: a fetal angel of white-hot plasma, curled, its wings folded, a cord of fire running from it to the cage, its heart beating through the whole room. The dying star's own heart. Three phases: the cage, the waking, the end.

idle
  • Phase one: the flares. Gold field lines arc out of the cage's poles down to the floor. One after another a line brightens, dots running down it, its splash spot ringed; then a ball of fire runs down it and bursts on the floor in pillars of solar fire. They come in a rhythm, left and right; read the line, not the fire. One catches her: scorched.
  • Phase two: the waking. The rings strain, buckle and crack, sparks flying, focus lines closing in; the child stirs. The cage breaks with an impact frame, its rings flung across the room. Then, in one black-and-white frame, it opens its eyes for the first time: huge, white, tiny black pupils. Gravity turns toward it: gold arrows pointing in, grit spiralling in, and every platform falls into orbit round it, Elfy riding hers.
  • Phase three: the countdown. Twelve ticks round it go out one by one, and it burns brighter. The platforms glow red-hot and burn away into embers one by one, until only hers is left.
  • Collapse. Its desperation move: an implosion. The edges of the room go dark, everything streaks inward, her platform is dragged toward it. The pull is the only way in: she rides it straight to the core and strikes. A comic star, a white impact frame, three shockwaves.
  • Weak window. It curls up small, dims and cracks, smoking, its eyes closing. Then the platforms and the cage re-form out of the embers.
