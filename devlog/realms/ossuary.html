Realm 7: Noon at the Ossuary Chapel

Gothic, undead and poisoned, and dressed for a wedding at high noon. A chapel built inside an ossuary: every wall is bones, skulls stacked in niches and femurs laid in herringbone, all of it painted in sorbet pastels, blush and lilac and butter and cream. Sun pours through a rose window in long shafts full of dust; a lilac runner runs down the aisle between chequered marble; there are garlands, a candle in every niche, petals hanging in the air, and three balconies piped like the tiers of a cake. The elements are new: bone, mirror-light, wine, wax, ink and sugar. Poison is violet and magenta; blades warn in white. Realm 6: the orrery.

1 · Procession, Toss, Skull Volley, the Tower Ram, Lid Slam · bone, four as one

The Pallbearers

Four skeletons in lilac morning coats, boutonnieres and veiled top hats, carrying a blush-lacquered coffin with gold handles on their shoulders, always in step. Something inside the coffin is poisoned and breathing. They are one enemy with four bodies, and the coffin they carry is a platform: you can ride it, and they will use that.

idle
  • Always in step. Four skulls bob together under their top hats, coat-tails swinging, the coffin rocking on their shoulders; their eye sockets glow violet.
  • The procession. They lower their heads and the lane lights up the length of the room; then they charge, marble chips flying. Standing in the lane is death, but the coffin is the safe place: Elfy jumps onto it and rides it to the wall. The stop is a hit-stop and a flash.
  • The toss. They crouch, and a violet fence marks where the poison will fall; Elfy double-jumps off the coffin for the high tier. They heave it straight up: it flips end over end, the lid swings open and violet poison pours out of it in a curtain, and they catch it again.
  • The skull volley. Each pops its own head off and lobs it at the high tier, one after another, each landing ringed first. The skulls bounce, rattle and come rolling home to their necks. Elfy leaves the tier for the right-hand one.
  • The tower ram (phase two). They climb onto each other's shoulders, two towers holding the coffin high as a battering ram, its line drawn at tier height. Elfy drops to the floor under it; the ram sweeps the tier she was on.
  • Lid slam. The towers topple forward, the landing outlined and the floor turning violet, and bring the coffin down lid first. A wave of poison gas rolls out both ways: jump it.
  • Weak window. They fall apart into a heap of bones round the overturned coffin, four skulls rolling on the marble, eyes dimmed, stars over them. Elfy cuts them three times; then they put themselves back together, lift the coffin, and she has to jump all four of them to get home.
2 · Lunge, Noon Beam, Shatter, Every Mirror · mirror-light

Lady Gloss

A vampire countess who exists only in mirrors. In the room she is a gap where a woman should be; in the glass she is all there: a black-violet ballgown with a white lace hem, a corset laced in blush, a wired lace collar standing up behind her head, silver hair piled high with a ribbon, red eyes, a black lace fan. Two gilt mirrors hang on the walls and a cheval glass stands under the high tier, and she moves between them.

idle
  • Only in the glass. Each mirror shows a dim silver room and a slanting highlight; she appears in one, fanning herself, and is simply not there in the room. When she comes out, she is glassy, glints trailing her, and goes back in with a splash of glitter.
  • The lunge. She appears in the right-hand mirror and her path is dotted: out, down across the floor, up into the left-hand mirror. She comes out fan first, horizontal, and flies it. Dash under the low point.
  • The noon beam. She raises her fan in the cheval glass and catches the sun from the rose window; the beam's zigzag is dotted from mirror to mirror and down to the floor. Then a bar of white light burns along it, flaring at each bounce, scorching the marble where it lands. Stand where it doesn't go.
  • The shatter. She presses her hands to the glass and it cracks into a spider; rings mark the floor. Then the cheval glass bursts and the shards fly up in a fan, turning, and fall on their rings. The glass mends itself after.
  • Every mirror (phase two). She is in all three mirrors at once, and three of her lunge at Elfy together. Only one casts a shadow on the floor: the real one. The other two are glass; dash through them and they burst into glitter. Jump the real one as it skids along the floor and dives for the cheval glass.
  • Weak window. She dives back into the glass too fast and sticks in it to the waist, her skirts kicking in the room, the glass cracked round her; Elfy cuts her three times before the mirror swallows her back.
3 · The Pour, the Cork, the Corkscrew, the Wine Serpent · wine

The Sommelier

An undead wine steward with impeccable manners: bony legs in black trousers and polished shoes, a long white apron, a silver tray of coupes on one hand and a corkscrew for the other. His body is a cut-glass decanter full of poisoned wine, sloshing whenever he moves, violet bubbles rising in it; his skull is its stopper, with a monocle on a chain and a waxed moustache.

idle
  • The pour. He bows, the floor ahead of him goes wine-dark, then he tips himself over like a decanter and pours: a stream from his neck, and the floor floods from his feet to the far wall with poisoned wine, bubbling violet. It soaks away after. Be up.
  • The cork. He shakes himself up, the wine foaming inside him, and the cork's whole ricochet is dotted with every bounce ringed: ceiling, the left tier, the wall, home. Then his head pops off with a bang and a geyser of froth and ricochets along it. Drop off the tier before it gets there.
  • The corkscrew. His corkscrew arm winds up, whirring, its line drawn at chest height across the room; then he lunges, the arm stretching the length of the floor in a shower of sparks. Get high.
  • The wine serpent (phase two). The decanter cracks and the wine leaves him: a ribbon of poisoned wine with his skull for a head, swimming the room in a figure of eight, its loop shown faintly. It turns on the high tier, its landing outlined; drop off, and it dives past into the floor and bursts up as three geysers where the rings were.
  • Weak window. The empty decanter slumps on its legs, dry and cracked, the monocle dangling, a drip at the neck. Elfy cuts him three times; then the wine runs back up into him and he re-corks himself.
4 · Drips, the Flood, the Wick Chain, the Spray, the Waxwork · wax

Taper, the Waxwork Saint

A saint made of a hundred melted altar candles: a tapering column of layered wax in cream, blush and lilac, robes carved into it and running, drips forever creeping down it; a melted saint's face with hollow eyes that weep wax; and for a crown, a ring of lit wicks. It holds up a candelabra. Everything it does leaves wax behind, and the wax is part of the fight.

idle
  • Drips. It tilts its crown and rings mark the floor; drips of hot wax fly from the wicks and land on them, and harden there into lumps.
  • The flood. It sinks into itself, melting at the base, and the floor glows: hot in orange, the safe islands outlined in white. Then molten wax runs across the whole floor, glowing and steaming, except the islands, which stand proud. It cools from the islands outward into hardened cream.
  • The wick chain. It raises the candelabra and a row of wicks sparks along the floor; then they light one after another, pillars of candle-flame chasing across the room. Go up.
  • The spray (phase two). Its crown flares to twice the height and it fills its cheeks; a cone is drawn from its mouth over the high tier. Drop and crouch: a fan of molten wax sprays over where Elfy was.
  • The waxwork (after the pixelfx summon-rise). The spilt wax stands up as a waxwork of Elfy, her own shape in cream, its lane lit; it lunges at her. Jump it, and it sags back into a puddle.
  • Weak window. It sprayed so hard it blew its own crown out: the wicks gutter and smoke, it slumps on itself, the wax cracking. Elfy cuts it three times; then a spark, and the crown relights.
5 · Dripping Words, Grasping Hands, the Hex, the Blotted Sun · ink and shadow

The Scrivener

The registrar of the dead, who signs every guest in: a plague doctor in a long ink-black coat bleeding at the hem, a capelet with bone buttons, a wide hat; a bone mask with round lilac lenses whose long beak is a quill, its nib always wet; a scroll of names unrolled to the floor. It fights by writing, and everything it writes comes true.

idle
  • Dripping words. It nods its head, scribbling, and a line writes itself across the air under the ceiling, dotted and flickering, the floor under it marked. Then the ink lets go: a curtain of drops falls from the line and spatters the floor. Get out from under; the left balcony is a roof.
  • Grasping hands (after the pixelfx grasping-hands). It flicks three blots onto the floor, each ringed. A hand of ink grasps up out of each, one after another, fingers curling, lit violet. Be up on the balcony.
  • The hex (after the pixelfx hex-sigil). It points, and a hex writes itself on the balcony under Elfy: a ring of turning runes and a star in it. Get off; it goes up in a column of ink.
  • The blotted sun (phase two) (after the pixelfx eclipse and shadow tendrils). It flings ink over the rose window and the room goes black; only Elfy, its lenses and its shadow are lit. The shadow comes off the floor: a long arm of shadow sweeps the length of the floor (a line flickers first; jump), then again at head height (crouch). The light comes back.
  • Weak window. It slips in its own ink and goes down on its back, its quill-beak stuck in the floor, stars over it. Elfy cuts it three times; then it pulls its beak free and dusts itself off.
6 · Bouquet, Poppers, the Hop, the Wheel, the Crawl · sugar · the bride

The Widow Cake

The bride, and the last of the wedding. A three-tier wedding cake in blush and cream, piped scallops and running icing, sugar skulls round every tier, a violet ribbon, a lit candle every few inches; and for its topper the bride herself: a skeleton in a lace bodice under a veil that pours down the cake to the floor, a tiara, a bouquet of poison-violet roses. The fight takes the cake apart tier by tier until she comes for you herself.

idle
  • The bouquet toss (after the pixelfx charmed status). She raises the bouquet and its landing is ringed; she throws it in a high arc, and where it lands it bursts into a haze of poison and floating hearts that lingers. It is very pretty and you must not walk into it.
  • The poppers (after the pixelfx confetti). Her candles flare and the floor in front of the cake is fenced off; then every candle goes off like a party popper, three fans of sugar sprinkles arcing onto the floor and confetti everywhere. Be up on a balcony.
  • The hop. The whole cake squats, its landing outlined, then jumps and comes down with a crash of icing and rings along the floor.
  • The wheel (phase two). The bottom tier cracks off; its lane is lit at wheel height. It rolls the length of the room, spokes of ribbon turning; double jump it.
  • The crawl (phase three). The bride drags herself out of the cake, drops to the floor and crawls at Elfy on her bony hands, veil dragging, the lane lit. She rears, the lunge's arc dotted, and throws herself; Elfy flips over her.
  • Weak window. She lands face down in her own veil, confetti everywhere, stars over her. Elfy cuts her three times; then she crawls home and the cake makes itself whole again.
